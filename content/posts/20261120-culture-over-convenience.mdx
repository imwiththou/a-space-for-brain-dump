The more I travelled, the more I rethought the relationship between human and nature, the history and the culture. It appears that there’s an increasing tendency for both me and Joseph to be detached from the mainstream view and notion of travel. 

It’s funny that reports show more Singaporeans are travelling to destinations such as Tokyo, Osaka and Kyoto in Japan; Shanghai, Beijing and Chongqing in China; Bangkok and Chiangmai in Thailand; Taipei and Kaohsiung in Taiwan. As if these are the default destinations for them whenever a country is mentioned. It’s a lazy way to plan a trip just in the name of “oh it’s family friendly”. 

Of course the definition of travel, especially luxurious travel, can vary among persons, it’s just that the mainstream value of a trip in Singaporeans’ mindset really goes against what we think of travel. For the most, travel is just about staying in a luxury hotel, porting over largely the man-made convenience from Singapore to another country, that includes, but not limited to, a prime and accessible location, handful choices of shopping malls and restaurants, theme and amusement parks for the kids, etc. If we define that as the requirements of a holiday destination, then what makes an economically and touristically developed spot different from another? We narrow our travel mindset just like most Americans: shallow, money-god oriented, impulsive shopping, carousel-like checking in locations to show off on social media later. All these reflect how careless we are when it comes to culture, history and art. We don’t design our trip to experience other countries, we design our trip so that we feel living in the exact same country as much as we could

How pathetic is that mindset, when we socially collectively lack taste? The taste of good art, a good conversation, a good walk in the forest and perhaps, a good one hour or two in a park with a book and hot chocolate on the bench, phone-free while enjoying the winter sun, the apricity lightens up the chill brought by wintry plunge from Arctic air as I set my footpath in the early winter along the Alps. Rarely will Singaporeans think of that as the vision of travelling because, these are inconvenient. Inconvenience by nature it’s not challenging at all naturally, it’s just how things are. When we are too normalised with man-made convenience we don’t really feel the joy of reaching a destination after walking a few blocks on pebble-stone-paved roads, probably dropping by a few random shops along the way and found some surprises in there. We make things move so fast that thinking independently is regarded as almost a crime for being against the flow, for being not productive and efficient enough, but what’s the fun if everything is about getting faster and faster, making soulless decisions and choices, that’s not what makes us human.
